Ask any accountant what eats their month-end, and bank reconciliation is usually near the top. For a small business it matters even more: your bank statement is the one record you cannot edit, so it is the anchor of truth for your books. This guide explains the process in plain terms.

## What reconciliation actually is

Bank reconciliation is comparing two lists of the same money:

1. **Your books (cash/bank ledger)** — every receipt and payment you recorded
2. **The bank statement** — every transaction the bank actually processed

The goal is to explain every difference between the two, line by line, until the adjusted balances agree.

## Why the balances differ

The differences are almost always one of these:

- **Timing differences** — a cheque you issued that hasn't been presented yet, or a customer payment recorded today that the bank credits tomorrow
- **Bank-only entries** — bank charges, interest credited or debited, payment-gateway fees, ECS/NACH debits you forgot to record
- **Errors** — a transposed figure (₹5,430 entered as ₹4,530), a duplicate entry, or a payment posted to the wrong ledger
- **Missing entries** — sales collected by UPI or card that never made it into the books at all

A growing unreconciled gap usually means missing entries, and that is where leakage and fraud hide.

## The step-by-step process

1. **Fix the period.** Reconcile a defined window — usually a month.
2. **Start from the last reconciled balance.** If you've never reconciled, start from a date where the book and bank balances are known to agree (often the account opening date or financial year start).
3. **Tick matched items.** Go through the bank statement line by line and match each entry to a book entry. Most accounting software automates the obvious matches by amount and date.
4. **List unmatched bank entries.** Bank charges, interest, gateway fees, auto-debits — record these in your books now.
5. **List unmatched book entries.** Cheques not yet presented and deposits in transit stay on the reconciliation statement as timing items.
6. **Investigate everything else.** Whatever is left is an error or a missing entry. Resolve it; don't park it.
7. **Document the result.** The reconciliation statement should show: book balance, plus/minus timing items, equals bank balance.

## How often should a small business reconcile?

Monthly is the minimum that keeps GST and income-tax filings safe. But if your business takes daily UPI, card and cash payments — a kirana counter, a pharmacy, a distributor — **weekly or even daily reconciliation** is realistic with software support and catches problems while they are still small. A missed gateway settlement is easy to chase after three days and very hard after three months.

## Habits that make it painless

- Record sales and payments the day they happen, not at month-end
- Use separate ledgers for each bank account and each payment gateway
- Keep personal and business accounts strictly separate
- Chase unpresented cheques older than a couple of months — they may need reversal
- Treat the bank statement import (CSV/PDF/feed) as the starting point, and let software propose matches you confirm
